### 🕒 4:30 PM — The Hackathon Begins (Solo Queue)
The hackathon officially kicked off at 4:30 PM. Meanwhile, my teammate **Aswal** took his sweet time to spawn because bro went for university badminton selections where he got humbled. *(Don't worry, he's taking the W on Monday).*

So I sat down and started speed-brainstorming ideas with **DeepSeek** *(DeepSeek is peak, by the way — fastest responses in the game)*. My initial goal was to build a software project that leveraged unusual hardware already present in laptops and smartphones.

---

### 💻 5:15 PM — The Failed Laptop Hinge Experiment
My first idea was a **hinge-based physics game** where moving your laptop screen would control game physics. It quickly turned into an absolute calibration nightmare trying to detect micro-hinge angles via the webcam. I realized early that this was a trap.

Time kept ticking. Between asking Claude, DeepSeek, and ChatGPT for inspiration, 6:00 PM rolled around with zero code committed.

---

### 🖱️ 6:00 PM — The Teammate Spawns & The Screaming Cursor
At 6:00 PM, Aswal finally spawned at the venue.

Bro pulled up with a concept for a chaotic, unhinged mouse cursor that loses its mind and screams at you if you don't touch your mouse for 5 seconds. We decided to split our bets — he started cooking on his cursor idea in C#/WPF while I kept searching for something even dumber.

---

### 🍛 8:00 PM — TinkerHub Community Break & The Veg Curry Rating
At 8:00 PM, the TinkerHub organizers and volunteers pulled everyone away from their laptops for a community event. We got dragged around to the basketball court, took a mental breather, and ate dinner:
- **Chapati**: W
- **Veg Curry**: 2/10 *(respectfully)*

---

### 💡 9:00 PM — The Epiphany: "Why Don't We Let the Phone Pose?"
After dinner, the realization hit me: Every camera app in human history exists for people to pose in front of it. Humans tilt their heads, strike flattering angles, and pout. Meanwhile, the smartphone — with its precision gyroscopes, magnetometers, and lenses — is held dead-flat like an obedient serving tray.

**What if, for once in history, the phone gets to strike dramatic poses and refuses to take photos until the human obeys?**

We officially kicked off development.

---

### 🛠️ 11:00 PM — Prototyping at Light Speed with Antigravity & ADB
Using **Google Antigravity IDE** paired with USB Debugging and live ADB hot-deployments on physical Android hardware, the core TiltShift\* prototype came together rapidly:
- Fused `Sensor.TYPE_ROTATION_VECTOR` tracking real-time pitch, azimuth, and zoom.
- Live tactile shutter locks.
- Real-time savage roast engine insulting your crooked posture.

---

### 👨‍🏫 1:00 AM — Mentor Infiltration & The Merger
Mentors started circling the tables to review progress. We showed both demos:
1. Aswal demoed the screaming cursor.
2. I demoed the locked **TiltShift\*** camera demanding 45° tilt and 270° West alignment with savage roasts.

The TiltShift\* demo was such absolute fire and comedic gold that Aswal immediately scrapped the cursor project and merged forces onto TiltShift\* *(as we should have from the beginning!)*.

---

### 🎨 3:00 AM — The Sprite Sheet Loading Screen Hustle
We wanted a hand-drawn doodle aesthetic. We created a custom stickman photographer with dynamic crayon loading bar sprites:
- Setting up the multi-frame sprite sheet animation in Jetpack Compose was a **PAIN IN THE A\*\*** to calibrate.
- But with Antigravity's rapid UI iterations, it turned out gorgeous.

Aswal spent the graveyard shift grinding through UI polish, Lucide vector icons, "Baby Mode" semi-transparent assistive reticles, and the **Useless Peaktures Gallery** browser.

---
